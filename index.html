08 — Philosophy

Why
immersive learning

Engineering education has always had a gap: students graduate knowing the theory but uncertain about what the real job actually looks like — a construction site, a power plant, an industrial workshop. These are environments you can't bring into a classroom.

I believe VR closes that gap. When a student can step into a simulated job site, make decisions under pressure, and learn from mistakes without real risk — they build intuition and confidence no textbook provides. That acceleration is what drives my work.

My background in engineering research and a year teaching Physics gives me both the technical depth and the pedagogical instinct to build experiences with real educational value — not just spectacle.

1
Research-backed simulations
My VR environments are built on real physics — COMSOL simulation data, IEEE-published research, and FEM modeling. The immersion is artistic; the underlying science is rigorous.
2
Pedagogy first, technology second
VR for its own sake is spectacle. I design learning objectives first, then build the immersive experience around them — informed by a year in the classroom.
3
Access over prestige
The most urgent need for immersive engineering education is in universities that can't afford physical labs. I build with accessibility and equity as core constraints.
4
Theory → simulation → confidence
My pipeline: take a real engineering scenario, build it in Unity, let students repeat it until it feels familiar. Real-world confidence follows naturally.
